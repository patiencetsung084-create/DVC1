Science Alive Museum

A new home for hands-on science in Christchurch, designed for 245 Manchester Street.

Why this building

The original Science Alive building in Christchurch has been closed since the 2011 earthquake. This design brings the museum back to the city centre, giving families and school groups a place to explore science again.

The site

I chose 245 Manchester Street in the Central City over two other options, 111 Moorhouse Avenue and 18 Stewart Street. It sits in the Innovation Precinct, close to the Avon River corridor, so the building can stand out as a modern landmark while still feeling connected to its surroundings.

Inside the building

The brief called for six key spaces. Reception sits directly beside the entrance, and the office is placed next to the sensory room.

EntranceThe welcoming first moment of the visit.
ReceptionRight beside the entrance for easy arrival.
Science tech spaceThe main space for science exhibits.
CafeKitchen planned around a triangle work area of sink, fridge, oven, coffee machine, dishwasher and till.
Sensory roomA calmer space, placed beside the office.
Office and toiletsSupporting spaces for staff and visitors.

Materials and colour

The exterior uses aluminium cladding and aluminium roofing. The colour palette picks up the sky and glazing, the nearby river, and the reflective quality of the aluminium.
